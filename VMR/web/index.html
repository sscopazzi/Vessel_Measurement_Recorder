Waves · from the ship's motion

 
--

 

  Encounter period-- pitching period
  Roll period-- roll spectrum peak
  Heave Hs-- 
  Pressure Hs-- 
  Fused Hs-- 
 

 
 
Learned pressure gain 

 
 

 

  

   
   
   
   
  

  

  

 

 
Worked out every 30 s from the last 17 min of motion: the first after 8.5 min, again after a pause or IMU gap, off at the dock. An old estimate stays, marked LAST ESTIMATE with its age.

 
Encounter period: the peak of the pitch spectrum, the period the ship meets the waves at. Roll period: the peak of the roll spectrum, the natural roll (GM uses it). The GPS & motion card's roll period times the rolls one by one, so it also takes in rolling forced by the waves.

 
Heave Hs: the ship's up-and-down motion (4 × its standard deviation, 1.6–20 s) from the vertical acceleration. Pressure Hs: the same motion from the BMP388 - 1 m is about 0.12 mbar, read 10 times a second, same window and band, no integration, so it holds up better in long swell; wind gusts, doors and ventilation reach it too, and it needs the BMP388 on for the whole 17 min. Fused Hs: the two together, period by period. Their cross-spectrum keeps the motion they share, so each one's own noise (wind on the pressure; noise and tilt on the acceleration) averages out. The pressure's gain against the motion is measured period by period, wherever the two agree (coherence 0.5 or more). At those periods the fused spectrum is the motion's own. Elsewhere it is the motion's up to 10 s and, from 14 s (long swell, where the acceleration grows weak), the shared part divided by the gain at the nearest period where they agree, blended between. Beside it: coherence, 0 to 1, how far the two agree (weighted by the motion); and how many times the motion the pressure reads at 12 s and shorter (×1.0 = the same). A pressure reading well above ×1 and in step with the motion is something moving with the ship, such as the wind on the bridge as it rolls. That is not removed: it is why the gain is measured where the motion is sound. Needs both for the whole window.

 
With the IMU off (Add-ons): the pressure alone, as Pressure Hs and its peak period at the VMR (no midships: that needs the pitch and roll), and no spectra. The large figure is corrected: the pressure's spectrum divided, period by period, by the gain learned while the IMU was on (kept on the microSD card, so it holds through restarts; the date and the gain at 12 s and shorter are beside it), with the raw figure alongside. The gain is the VMR's at its place on board: after moving it, run with the IMU on for a while so it learns again. Until a gain has been learned the raw figure is shown, labelled raw. Not while Datalogger sleeps between rows (1 min and slower), as the BMP388 is read only while the VMR is awake.

 
Learned pressure gain: how many times the motion's height the pressure reads, period by period, learned while the IMU is on wherever the two agree (a dot for each period with one; nothing is drawn between periods without one). It is kept on the microSD card and corrects Pressure Hs with the IMU off. Forget learned gain clears it, for example after moving the VMR. Show advanced: the coherence from which the two count as agreeing (0.5), the period to which Fused Hs is the motion's alone (10 s) and from which it is the shared part alone (14 s), and how far each 17-min window moves the learned gain (20 %). The defaults suit most ships; change them only to test the method.

 
With the VMR's position in the Level zero and ship data card, the large figures are at midships (the pitch and roll taken off) and the VMR's own is beside them; without it, the VMR's alone. All are the ship's heave, not the wave height: a large ship moves less than the waves in a short sea.

 
The graph shows the heave, pitch and roll spectra by period, each scaled to its own peak; the period axis fits the data (2 s either side of where any curve is at least 5 % of its peak, within 2–35 s), as on the OLED; the larger of roll and pitch is boxed in the legend. Reset clears the estimates and starts the 8.5 min again (after a course or speed change, say). Save image saves the card as a PNG.

Bottle events

Six Niskin bottles, closed by the IWB's solenoid driver from a 10,000 µF capacitor. A bottle fires only when it is armed, a CTD cast is open (the Cast session card) and the capacitor is charged; the green LED on the WTB and the chip at the top say it is charged. After a firing the capacitor recharges from the 12 V: the bar and the countdown show it.
Arm a bottle once it is cocked: its ring lights on the WTB. Fire here (it asks first) or hold its button on the WTB 1.5 s - the ring blinks while held, goes out when the bottle has fired, and flickers if the bottle can't fire now (the reason is under Bottle events). A fired bottle shows when, the depth, temperature, salinity and density it closed at, and what fired it. The firing is also saved in the cast's _bottles.csv on the WTB, in the profile file's header, and as a location mark (the Location marks card). Fired? no answer means the IWB never confirmed: check the bottle. Safe stops a bottle firing (button or planned depth); Reset puts a fired bottle back to safe for the next cast and clears it from this card (the files and the mark keep it).
Planned depths: an armed bottle with a planned depth fires on the way down when the CTD reaches it. If the capacitor isn't charged in time and the CTD goes 1 m past, the bottle is left armed with an event. Two planned depths must be far enough apart for the capacitor to recharge between them at the lowering speed: at least the speed × the recharge time × 2, and never under 0.5 m (the recharge time is the IWB's last measured one, 1 s until it has one).

Wi-Fi hotspot: press to switch it on; switching it off asks first, as Record. Screen jump: press to show the OLED screen picked in Jumps to (the number top right of each screen), press again to go back. In Datalogger mode any of them wakes the VMR.
The PCF8574 INT wire goes to Feather pin 5. Pin 9 (OLED button A's; A and C are not used) takes an LED. Feather A5 takes an LED or a backlight colour only with the wind meter off.
Piezo (the alarms' sound, card 23): a bare piezo disc or a passive piezo buzzer (Adafruit 1739 or 1740), not one with its own driver. One leg to the pin picked as Piezo +, the other to GND (Piezo − None); or the other leg to a second free pin as Piezo −, which drives it in opposite phase: twice the voltage across it, noticeably louder. No resistor. It plays 2.65 kHz, near where most discs are loudest. Not on a pin an LED, backlight colour, optional button, the wind meter, the SeaLabMOS relay or the laptop port uses.

Battery, 3.7 V LiPo: on the battery the % is from the cell voltage, through a discharge curve measured on a 2500 mAh Adafruit LiPo (0 % = 3.30 V, 20 % = 3.575 V): the fuel gauge's own % reads far low on these cells. Time left is the % over how fast it has fallen over up to the last 4 h, started again whenever the VMR changes its own load (Wi-Fi, Datalogger, CPU speed): shown after 20 min, and until then from the draw measured last time; with the capacity entered, that rate also gives the draw in mA. On USB, and for 3 min after it comes out, the gauge's own % and rate: a charging cell sits near 4.2 V whatever it holds. USB going in or out is seen from a jump in battery voltage; jumps within 30 s of the VMR changing its own load (Wi-Fi, CPU speed, displays, GPS) are ignored. Low (the Fault LED) is under 20 %, and clears only once it is really charging. At 0 % (3.30 V for a minute) the VMR saves everything and shuts down: the recording gets a BATTERY EMPTY row and is closed, a cast session is disarmed, settings are saved, then it switches everything off and stays off: charge it, then switch it off and on. The OLED's Health screen shows the same. Capacity: the battery's Ah from its label (0 or blank: not set).

 
Battery, 12 V (INA260, with SeaLabMOS): volts and amps measured, + charging. The % is counted from the amps against the capacity, started from the chemistry's resting voltage and reset to full when charging tapers; with no capacity, the voltage alone. Low: under 20 %, or at the chemistry's floor (LiFePO4 12.0 V, Li-ion 3S 10.8 V, lead-acid 11.9 V), never while charging.

 
Units: temperatures in °F or °C, speeds in knots, m/s or km/h, distances in nautical miles or km, and lengths in feet or metres everywhere - the OLED, 7-segment, LCD and this page (default °F, knots, nautical miles and feet). Speeds include the wind's; the route's planned speeds and the VRM are typed in the units shown. In feet, drafts are typed as feet-inches (33-04, or 33-04.5) or decimal feet (33.33), as Lengths says; they show as feet-inches (the trim calibration's to a tenth of an inch, the ship data's to the inch). The CSV files keep their own units, named in each column.

 
Show hidden files (this phone): the microSD files card gets a Hidden tab with the VMR's own files - the chart track, the learned wave gain, ship saves - to look at or download. They are not deleted from there.

 
Settings backups: Back up settings saves every setting (pins, add-ons, displays, ship data, level zero, alarms, units...) under a name in a hidden folder on the microSD card (.cfg). Restore puts one back and restarts the VMR; the all-time maxima stay as they are. A backup from firmware with a different settings layout is refused (the list marks it). Handy before Clear EEPROM in a firmware update, or to set up a second VMR the same: copy the .cfg folder across.

 
Restart VMR: the log file is closed first and settings are kept; this page reconnects by itself.

 
Wi-Fi name: the hotspot is called VMR and the name typed here (up to 21 letters, digits, spaces and . _ - ( ) #), or VMR and this unit's 4-character tag with the box empty. Save restarts the hotspot under the new name: every phone drops off and has to join the new network, with the same password. A settings restore keeps this unit's name.

 
Restart Wi-Fi: phones drop off for a few seconds and rejoin. Use it if a phone can't join (the hotspot takes 4).

 
Devices: name this phone in the box below the list so it shows by name.

 
Reset I2C bus: use it if roll and pitch freeze.

 
Reset maxima: erases the all-time maxima (the Maxima, roll statistics and GM card).

 
Clear EEPROM: erases everything the VMR keeps in its EEPROM - every setting (pin mappings, add-ons, displays, ship data, level zero, trim calibration, alarms, track settings, units...) and the all-time maxima - and restarts it into the factory defaults. A recording is stopped first. Files on the microSD card are not touched (recordings, marks, the track, settings backups). Back up settings first (Settings backups, this card) to be able to put them back.

 
Memory and web timing are for diagnosing slow updates. Slow updates with short send times mean a weak Wi-Fi signal: move closer.

Where the VMR is now (red), its track over the time picked (1 h up to the hours the VMR keeps, 72 by default: the Charts card; per phone; its colour from the Track swatches, per phone, red at night), and the location marks made in that time (numbered as in the Location marks card; a cast shows its start, and its end as a ring), and the active route (the Route card, on the Chart tab). The VMR keeps a track point every 30 s while it moves (15 m or more) and every 5 min while it is still, only with a GPS fix and the clock set, on its microSD card, so a restart keeps it; it holds the last 72 h, or what the Charts card's Track settings say (up to all of it). A span with more than 50,000 points is thinned to about 50,000 and the line under the chart says so; Export track saves what is drawn. Clear track empties it for every phone (the marks stay).
Anchor: Drop anchor here starts the anchor watch at the GPS position now and draws its swing circle. The position is locked: the small lock opens it to type a position (decimal degrees, - for south and west) and Set; Drop again here moves it to the GPS position now. Set the radius and Set radius. The VMR keeps watching with no phone connected, and the alarm (card 23, output Visual or louder) goes when the GPS is farther from it than the radius, or has had no fix for a minute. The circle is centred on the GPS antenna, not the anchor: it suits small craft, where the two are close. On a large ship the antenna can be a whole ship length from the hawse pipe, so the circle has to be made that much larger, and swinging alone can set it off. Anchor up turns the watch off. Lines are never drawn across a gap: a break means the VMR was off or had no fix.
With chart packs on the VMR (Setup › Charts) it is NOAA's nautical chart there, with no internet needed; it is not for navigation. With internet on this phone or laptop as well (mobile data, say), street, satellite or topo tiles and OpenSeaMap's buoys and lights too; the layers button picks the background. Drag and zoom it; Fit brings everything back into view. COG vector: the line ahead of the VMR along its course over ground, as far as it goes at its speed in the minutes set (0 to 120), with a tick every 6 min. Follow keeps the ship in view: drag the chart to put the ship where you want it (ahead of it showing more, as a radar's offset), Centre puts it back in the middle, Fit stops following. EBL/VRM: two bearing lines with range rings (1 green, 2 purple; the number button turns one on or off). Each starts at the ship, or offset: Dropped stays at a fixed point on the chart, Carried moves with the ship at the same bearing and distance from it (Place, then tap the chart where it starts). Tap the chart to set the highlighted one's bearing and range to that point, or type them. A laptop shows where the pointer is with its range and bearing from the ship (a phone: tap). Time marks along the track (local time) space themselves to the zoom. Export track saves the track shown as a GPX file. Pop out (a laptop): the chart in a window of its own, to sit beside the dashboard; the dashboard leaves it out until that window closes. With neither, it is a plain grid of latitude and longitude with a scale: drag it, pinch, scroll or double-tap to zoom, or use + and −.





 
Charts

 
NOAA nautical charts for the Chart tab, kept on the VMR's microSD card so they show with no internet.

 
Not for navigation. These are NOAA's electronic navigational charts (ENCs) turned into pictures, for situational awareness only. A pack shows NOAA's charts as they were when it was built (again each month NOAA's files change), so it can be missing recent corrections and Notices to Mariners; detail is left out at some zooms; and the ship drawn on it is the VMR's own GPS position, which can be wrong or late. The VMR is not an ECDIS or a type-approved chart system: navigate by the vessel's official charts and publications, and keep a proper lookout.

Load a chart pack

 
 
 
Recording: charts load only while not recording.

 

 

 
Chart packs: github.com/sscopazzi/Vessel_Measurement_Recorder/releases, under "Chart packs", made again whenever NOAA updates its charts (looked for each month).

 
On a phone or laptop with internet, download the chart packs you want (.vmrchart files, one per area; the list above shows the ones published and not on this VMR yet). Then join the VMR's Wi-Fi, pick a pack here and Load it; a 40 MB pack takes a few minutes, and the live data waits meanwhile. Only while not recording: starting a recording stops a load. Track: a point is kept every so many seconds while the VMR moves (more than the distance set since the last point), and every so many while it is still. The track is kept only on the microSD card (a hidden folder, .trk), for the span set (24 h to 30 days), or All: never cut back. The line under the settings gives its points and size now. A span is cut back once its oldest point is a quarter of the span past it. On the Chart card a span with more than 50,000 points is thinned to about 50,000 (every 2nd, 3rd... point, and the points either side of each gap); the line under the chart says so. Nothing is drawn across a gap. A pack with the same name as one on the VMR replaces it, so load a newer pack over the last. Each area comes two ways: standard (name.vmrchart), with detail kept along the coast and in harbours and left out offshore, for a smaller file; and max detail (name-max.vmrchart), every chart tile NOAA has for the area at every zoom, for when room on the card is no concern. The VMR keeps one of an area's two: loading either replaces the other.
Each pack holds the whole region at overview detail, the straits and sounds closer, and the harbours closest. Zoomed in past what a pack holds, the chart enlarges the closest level it has; where no pack covers, it shows nothing. NOAA's date is when NOAA last made its chart tiles for that area: the charts show NOAA's ENCs as of then, for knowing where you are, not for navigation. A pack for another area can be made on a laptop from NOAA's Chart Display Service files with charts/make_vmrchart.py in the VMR repository.
