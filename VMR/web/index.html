Waves · from the ship's motion

 
--

 

  Encounter period-- pitching period
  Roll period-- roll spectrum peak
  Heave Hs-- 
  Pressure Hs-- 
  Vessel heave avg--
 

 
 
Worked out every 30 s from the last 17 min of motion: the first after 8.5 min, again after a pause or IMU gap, off at the dock. An old estimate stays, marked LAST ESTIMATE with its age.

 
Encounter period: the peak of the pitch spectrum, the period the ship meets the waves at. Roll period: the peak of the roll spectrum, the natural roll (GM uses it). The GPS & motion card's roll period times the rolls one by one, so it also takes in rolling forced by the waves.

 
Heave Hs: the ship's up-and-down motion (4 × its standard deviation, 1.6–20 s) from the vertical acceleration. Pressure Hs: the same motion from the BMP388 - 1 m is about 0.12 mbar, read 10 times a second, same window and band, no integration, so it holds up better in long swell; wind gusts, doors and ventilation reach it too, and it needs the BMP388 on for the whole 17 min. Vessel heave avg: the mean of the two, shown only when both are there.

 
With the VMR's position in the Level zero and ship data card, the large figures are at midships (the pitch and roll taken off) and the VMR's own is beside them; without it, the VMR's alone. All are the ship's heave, not the wave height: a large ship moves less than the waves in a short sea.

 
The graph shows the heave, pitch and roll spectra by period, each scaled to its own peak; the period axis fits the data (2 s either side of where any curve is at least 5 % of its peak, within 2–35 s), as on the OLED; the larger of roll and pitch is boxed in the legend. Reset clears the estimates and starts the 8.5 min again (after a course or speed change, say). Save image saves the card as a PNG.

Bottle events

Six Niskin bottles, closed by the IWB's solenoid driver from a 10,000 µF capacitor. A bottle fires only when it is armed, a CTD cast is open (the Cast session card) and the capacitor is charged; the green LED on the WTB and the chip at the top say it is charged. After a firing the capacitor recharges from the 12 V: the bar and the countdown show it.
Arm a bottle once it is cocked: its ring lights on the WTB. Fire here (it asks first) or hold its button on the WTB 1.5 s - the ring blinks while held, goes out when the bottle has fired, and flickers if the bottle can't fire now (the reason is under Bottle events). A fired bottle shows when, the depth, temperature, salinity and density it closed at, and what fired it. Planned depths: an armed bottle with a planned depth fires on the way down when the CTD reaches it. If the capacitor isn't charged in time and the CTD goes 1 m past, the bottle is left armed with an event. Two planned depths must be far enough apart for the capacitor to recharge between them at the lowering speed: at least the speed × the recharge time × 2, and never under 0.5 m (the recharge time is the IWB's last measured one, 1 s until it has one).

Clear track empties it for every phone (the marks stay). Lines are never drawn across a gap: a break means the VMR was off or had no fix.
With chart packs on the VMR (Setup › Charts) it is NOAA's nautical chart there, with no internet needed; it is not for navigation. With internet on this phone or laptop as well (mobile data, say), street, satellite or topo tiles and OpenSeaMap's buoys and lights too; the layers button picks the background. Drag and zoom it; Fit brings everything back into view. COG vector: the line ahead of the VMR along its course over ground, as far as it goes at its speed in the minutes set (0 to 120), with a tick every 6 min. Follow keeps the ship in view: drag the chart to put the ship where you want it (ahead of it showing more, as a radar's offset), Centre puts it back in the middle, Fit stops following. EBL/VRM: a bearing line and a range ring from the ship; tap the chart to set both to a point, or type them. A laptop shows where the pointer is with its range and bearing from the ship (a phone: tap). Time marks along the track (local time) space themselves to the zoom. Export track saves the track shown as a GPX file. Pop out (a laptop): the chart in a window of its own, to sit beside the dashboard; the dashboard leaves it out until that window closes. With neither, it is a plain grid of latitude and longitude with a scale: drag it, pinch, scroll or double-tap to zoom, or use + and −.





 
Charts

 
NOAA nautical charts for the Chart tab, kept on the VMR's microSD card so they show with no internet. Not for navigation.

 

 

 

 
Load a chart pack

 
 
 
Recording: charts load only while not recording.

 

 

 
Chart packs: github.com/sscopazzi/Vessel_Measurement_Recorder/releases, under "Chart packs", made again whenever NOAA updates its charts (looked for each month).

 
On a phone or laptop with internet, download the chart packs you want (.vmrchart files: Strait of Juan de Fuca and Puget Sound, Washington outer coast, Southeast Alaska, San Francisco Bay to the Farallon Islands). Then join the VMR's Wi-Fi, pick a pack here and Load it; a 40 MB pack takes a few minutes, and the live data waits meanwhile. Only while not recording: starting a recording stops a load. A pack with the same name as one on the VMR replaces it, so load a newer pack over the last.
Each pack holds the whole region at overview detail, the straits and sounds closer, and the harbours closest. Zoomed in past what a pack holds, the chart enlarges the closest level it has; where no pack covers, it shows nothing. NOAA's date is when NOAA last made its chart tiles for that area: the charts show NOAA's ENCs as of then, for knowing where you are, not for navigation. A pack for another area can be made on a laptop from NOAA's Chart Display Service files with charts/make_vmrchart.py in the VMR repository.

Countdown on the 7-segment

 
Counts down MM:SS from the time you set (up to 99:59). The Recording LED blinks each second. At zero: BOOM and a light show on the 7-segment and the LEDs, then the 7-segment goes dark.
